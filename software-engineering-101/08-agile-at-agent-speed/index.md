## The lesson

Keep every scrum ceremony, but run the loop daily. Deploy continuously, ship features switched off behind a flag, and write down what you learned.

## The rule

Ship small changes continuously, once the automated checks pass. New features ship switched off, behind a flag. A person decides when each one turns on, for one client or a small group first. And end every session with a short written note on what was learned.

## Transcript

Software engineering one oh one. Lesson eight. Agile at agent speed.

### The sprint

Quick question. Why is a sprint two to four weeks long? Because somebody wrote it down as a rule? Because it's the smallest bet a team of people could afford to lose? Or because customers asked for it? The best answer is the second one. A couple of weeks was a cheap way to find out you were wrong.

A sprint runs on four ceremonies. Planning: decide what to build. The standup: where are we? The review: show what got built. And the retro: what would we change?

So now that agents do the building, which of those would I drop? None of them. I'd keep every one.

### The clock

What changes is the clock. When agents build in hours, the whole cycle fits in a day. Plan in the morning. Check in while it runs. Review in the afternoon. And a retro before you close the laptop. Every day. Every other day, at the slowest.

Because the ceremonies were never about the calendar. They're a loop. Decide. Check. Show. Learn. Agents just let you run it faster.

### Shipping

The second half is shipping. I believe in deploying all the time, with automated checks on every change. Daily, at the very least. Hourly is the ideal.

I know the objection. My feature takes two weeks. I can't ship half a feature. Yes, you can. You ship it switched off.

That's a feature flag. The code goes out every day, dark. When it's ready, a person turns it on for one client. Then a small group. Then everyone.

So deploying and releasing become two separate decisions. One is routine, gated by the automated checks. The other is a choice a person makes on purpose.

### The shift

And here's the shift underneath it all. The old risk was time. A wrong bet cost you weeks. With agents, a wrong bet mostly costs you usage. That's a much cheaper way to be wrong. So you can afford to find out sooner.

One habit makes all of this stick. End each session by writing down what you learned, somewhere the next agent will read it. A retro that isn't written down is just a conversation.

### The rule

Rule eight, for your agents. Ship small changes continuously, once the automated checks pass. New features ship switched off, behind a flag. A person decides when each one turns on, for one client or a small group first. And end every session with a short written note on what was learned.

### Your turn

Your turn. What's one thing you could ship switched off this week, instead of waiting until it's finished?
