How this book is edited

This is the editorial policy for Silicon Valley C Players. I'll keep it short, and I'll change this page when the process changes.

This page, and the clarified “AI writes essentially all the code” wording in the changelog, were added after feedback from @himanshu112-wq in issue #80. See Contributors.

Story chapters and the how-to half

The story chapters are an embellished fictional dramatization. Names are changed. The Foreword and Part Five (How to Work Now) are nonfiction: my own views, with real, cited sources. That split is also in the disclaimer.

Conflicting recollections

People remember the same days differently. If you were there and your memory does not match what's on the page, file a correction or share your side. I will read it. I may keep more than one account in view rather than quietly picking a winner. I will not invent a resolution I do not have.

Story chapters may still be fictionalized. Sending a recollection is not a promise that the published scene will match anyone's memory word for word.

How claims and sources are tracked

In the nonfiction half, a claim should have a source, or be clearly marked as my view. I will not treat "in my view" as the same thing as a measured result.

When a claim or a source in a published edition changes, it is recorded in the changelog below: what it used to say, what it says now, and which edition made the change.

The current public preview is the starting point for this log. This page is the policy going forward. I am not describing a formal process from earlier drafts.

Claims and sources changelog

This log starts with the current public preview on the site. Earlier preview files are kept in the repository under book-source/preview/archive/. When a later edition changes a published claim or source, it will be listed here.

Current public preview

  • Was: “In my view, AI now writes essentially all of the code,” and in the chapter 20 outline, “Clark's view is that AI now writes 100% of the code.”
  • Now: “In my own work, AI now writes essentially all of the code I ship. That is my workflow, not a measured team result or a general industry figure.”
  • Why: Issue #80, point 4, raised by @himanshu112-wq. Clark clarified this is his own workflow, not a measured result or a prediction.

Editorial policy (24 November 2026)

  • Why: Issue #80, point 7, raised by @himanshu112-wq.

Mid-book preview re-record (24 November 2026)

Privacy and analytics

This applies to the whole site, not only the book. The production site uses Google Analytics 4 to see which pages people read. IP addresses are anonymized. Google Signals and ads personalization are off, so the tag is not used for advertising. Watched-lesson progress, resume positions, and a last-visit timestamp stay in first-party cookies in your browser; they are not sent to Google. The last-visit cookie is used only to mark videos published or updated since your previous visit with a New tag. On a first visit, no New tags are shown. The comparison time is kept for the whole browsing session so the tags do not disappear when you open the next page. Pages under /staging/ do not send analytics hits.
