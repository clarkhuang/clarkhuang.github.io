## Chapter 36: Sick, Well, Fit

> “I strongly believe that AI is even more powerful to those that actually understand the fundamentals of the area they are applying AI to. Orders of magnitude more than the person vibing their way into a discipline AI gives them access to.” — Clark Huang

Clark Huang's view is that AI is far more powerful in the hands of people who understand the fundamentals of the area they apply it to than for someone vibing their way in, and this chapter gives that view a picture: Greg Glassman's line from sickness through wellness to fitness. Most companies aim for well, a normal reading; fit is a margin, and making AI part of how a company operates is hard enough to need one. The chapter carries CrossFit's models over to a company one by one (markers, the hopper, three engines, work capacity, mechanics then consistency then intensity, the pyramid, virtuosity, benchmarks, scaling, luck, open scoring) and ends with "Business Fitness in 100 Words" and a rule a person or an agent can follow.

### Business Fitness in 100 Words

> Spend on customers and product, people and tools, a little on overhead, nothing on vanity. Keep cash at levels that fund growth but not waste. Practice the core movements: build, sell, serve, collect. Master the basics: clean data, clear owners, written decisions, honest numbers. Ship, test, learn and fix, hard and fast. Every week, mix these in as many combinations as the work demands. Routine is the enemy; process is not. Keep projects short and intense. Put AI in every movement, mechanics first. Regularly take on problems you've never seen. Measure it, or it didn't happen.

Adapted from Greg Glassman's “World-Class Fitness in 100 Words” (CrossFit Journal, October 2002). Score your company against it:

> Read "Business Fitness in 100 Words" in https://clarkhuang.github.io/book/chapters/36-sick-well-fit/index.md (Chapter 36 of Clark Huang's free OpenBook, Silicon Valley C Players). Then score my company, or my team, against it, one line at a time. For each line: is it sick, well, or fit, and what evidence would prove that? Interview me one question at a time before you score; do not invent facts about my company. Finish with the two lines to work on first, one benchmark I could time every quarter, and the event I would least like to see come out of the hopper.
