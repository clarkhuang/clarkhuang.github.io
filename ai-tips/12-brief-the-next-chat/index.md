## The lesson

Long chats drift without warning: early decisions count for less and can fall out of view. When a chat gets long, have it write a handoff and carry on in a fresh chat.

## The rule

When a chat gets long, brief the next one: the goal and the limits, every decision so far, what's still open, and read it before you paste it.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

Long chats don't crash. They drift. And the decisions you made in the first hour quietly stop counting.

### The problem

Picture an afternoon planning a launch in one chat. In hour one, you set three limits. A capped budget. No paid ads. Launch on a Tuesday.

Hours later, you ask for the final plan. Over budget. Paid ads. Thursday. Nothing crashed, and nothing warned you.

### Why it happens

Here's what's going on. A model doesn't weigh a long chat evenly. Early messages tend to count for less as the chat grows, and some tools drop the oldest ones entirely. It won't tell you. It just keeps answering, from whatever it can still see.

### Where it shows up

And it's not just planning. A writing chat loses the style rules from the top. A coding session forgets a don't-touch-that-file rule. A research chat asks you something you settled an hour ago.

### The tell

So the tell is never an error message. It's a wobble. A question you already answered. A decision that comes back changed. A limit that quietly stops holding.

### The habit

When a chat gets long, or you feel that wobble, stop adding messages. Ask the chat for a handoff, written for a brand new chat that knows nothing. The goal, the limits, every decision, and what's still open. Then open a fresh chat, paste it in, and keep going there.

Here's the rule. When a chat gets long, don't wait for the wobble. Brief the next one. And read the briefing before you paste it, because a summary can drop things too.

Your turn. Open your longest running chat and ask it what you decided at the very start. Reply and tell me what it got wrong.

Long chats drift. Brief the next one.
