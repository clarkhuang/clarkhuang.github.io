## The lesson

If every Claude chat starts with you re-explaining your job and re-attaching the same file, a Project fixes it: shared knowledge and instructions in one place. This tip covers what sharing a project hands over (and what stays private), the permission choices, and where Cowork tasks run.

## The rule

When you find yourself re-uploading the same files or repeating the same instructions, put them in a Claude Project: files in project knowledge, standing rules in project instructions. Share the project with the people who'd benefit. Sharing hands over knowledge and instructions, while each person's chats stay private unless they share one deliberately. Choose org-wide or invite-only, and can-view or can-edit. If a task "can't see" your local folder, check whether it needs the desktop app open.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

Most people use Claude as a string of disconnected chats. Every time: who you are, what you do, and the same file, attached again.

### The idea

Every chat starts from nothing, so the brand guide goes in again. And again. A Project is one place that already holds it.

Two parts. Project knowledge is the files: upload them once, and every chat in the project can see them. Project instructions apply to every chat, too. Use our tone. We call it this, not that.

These videos come out of one project of mine. The context lives there, so each new one doesn't start from nothing.

### Sharing

Now the part most people don't know, and it's a big reason projects never get shared. The worry: but then everyone can read my chats.

They can't. Sharing hands over the knowledge and the instructions. Your chats stay private, even when the project is public. A chat is only shared if you share that one chat, on purpose.

Grok Bot's Team Bots have the same shape. Memory and skills are shared with everyone who uses one. Each person's chat stays private, and your connected accounts only ever act for you. Shared brain. Private seat.

When you share, two choices. Public to the whole organization, or private to the people you invite. And each person can view, or can edit.

Three small things that save time. Paste a whole list of email addresses at once. Projects shared with you land in their own tab. And archiving one keeps its members and permissions.

One caveat, to save you twenty minutes. Project sharing is a Team and Enterprise feature, and an administrator can switch it off. If you see that message, it isn't broken.

### Where it runs

And where the work runs. Cowork tasks run in the cloud, so you can close the laptop and they keep going. But anything that touches your own machine, local files, local connectors, the browser, still needs the desktop app open.

Which explains something people misread all the time. The task is running fine. It just can't see your folder right now.

Here's how I think about it. Asking a colleague for help gets you helped, once. Handing them your desk, your files and your notes helps everyone who sits down after you.

So share the project. Somebody's going to be glad you did.
