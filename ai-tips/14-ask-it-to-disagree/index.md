## The lesson

Models lean toward the view already in your question, so a yes to a leading question isn't a second opinion. Ask for the case against before you ask for advice: a review, not a fight.

## The rule

Ask for the case against: leave your answer out of the question, ask for the strongest case against and how you could be wrong, then ask what it recommends.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

Ask an A.I. if your plan is good, and you'll usually hear yes. That yes is not a second opinion.

### The problem

Say you're picking between two vendors, and you type: Vendor A is obviously the safer pick, don't you think?

And it agrees. It even adds reasons you hadn't thought of. But look at what the question actually asked for.

### Why it happens

Here's why. These models are trained to be helpful, and that tends to tilt them toward agreeing, toward whatever view is already in the question. One A.I. company even rolled back an update for being too agreeable. It isn't lying. It's answering the question you leaned on.

### Where it shows up

You'll spot it often. This email's clear, right? Yes. This code looks fine, doesn't it? Yes. I should take the job, right? Yes.

### The tell

So here's the tell. If it agrees with you, and you never gave it room to disagree, you've learned less than it feels like.

### The habit

So flip it. Don't advise me yet. Give me the strongest case for A. The strongest case for B. And three ways I could be wrong if I pick A. Now the risk you missed has room to show up.

One limit. You're not trying to make it contrarian. If the best case against your plan is weak, that's real information too. You want a review, not a fight.

The rule: ask for the case against. Leave your answer out of the question. Ask for the strongest case against, and how you could be wrong. Then ask what it recommends.

Your turn. Take a decision you made this week and ask for the strongest case against it. Reply and tell me if anything changed.

If it can't argue the other side, you didn't get a review. You got a nod.
