## The lesson

AI-supplied citations can be perfectly formatted and completely invented, and pushed on a dead link a model may insist the page changed. A reference you haven't opened is a claim, not a source.

## The rule

Open it before you use it: click the link, check the title and authors, find the sentence you need on the page. Nothing to open? Not a source yet.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

A tidy citation. Author, year, journal, even a link. It looks exactly like a source. Until you click it.

### The problem

Say you ask for studies to back up a point, and you get three tidy references. Open the first one. The link: page not found. The title: no such paper. The journal is real. The article never existed. The other two? Same check.

### Why it happens

Here's why. A model writes the most likely next words. A plausible author, a plausible title, a real journal. That's exactly what likely looks like. Whether the paper exists is a separate fact, and writing it doesn't check it.

### Where it shows up

And it isn't just homework. Courts have sanctioned lawyers over cases that were never decided, and over quotes that real cases never said. Professors grade papers whose links go nowhere.

And here's the part that catches people. Push back on a dead link, and a model will sometimes tell you the page must have changed. The accurate claim is smaller. It can't confirm the page ever existed.

### The tell

So here's the tell. If you're about to paste a reference you haven't opened yourself, you don't have a source. You have a claim about one.

### The habit

The habit is just a change of order. Not ask, paste, send. Ask, open, keep what's really there, and find the rest yourself.

The rule. Open it before you use it. Click the link. Check the title and the authors. Find the sentence you need, on the page itself. And if there's nothing to open, it isn't a source yet.

A citation is a claim. Open the source.
