## The lesson

A notetaker records one call and summarises it. A bot that's connected to your team's work can answer questions live, turn action items into real tickets, and remember what was decided last week. This tip shows how to try it on one meeting, and the limit that catches people out.

## The rule

Try a connected team bot on one recurring meeting, the one where someone always says "I'll check and get back to you." Invite it (or give it the link) and admit it from the waiting room. Afterwards, if you invited it, share the notes yourself, because often only the inviter receives them. Expect a first version: it may not see screen shares or speak aloud yet.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

If you rely on a meeting notetaker, here's what a bot in the meeting can do instead. And how to try it, once, this week.

### The difference

To be fair, notetakers do the job they were built for: record, summarize, stop. But a notetaker only has that one call. A bot that's connected to your team's work also sees things like the open tickets, the org chart, and last week's threads. Look at the difference.

One write-up you can act on. The other, you have to go and decode first, which is exactly the work you were trying to avoid.

### What it adds

That context buys three things a recording can't. During the call, ask it in the meeting chat for a number, or what a ticket actually says. After the call, action items become real tickets with an owner. And across calls, it remembers what was decided, and who promised what.

Picture a weekly huddle run this way. People ask for numbers live, while the discussion is happening. The meeting turns into a decision, instead of a status update.

### How to try it

Trying it is genuinely this short. Invite the bot to the meeting, and admit it from the waiting room. That's the whole setup. It can't invite itself, so the invite, or the link, has to come from you.

### The one that costs you

One limit actually costs people something. The bot can be shared while the notes are not. Often, only the person who invited it gets them.

It's the same shape as sharing a Claude Project: the tool is shared, the conversation isn't. And it's the done problem again. Meeting over, bot worked, notes exist, and your team still doesn't have them. If you invited it, passing them on is your job.

### What it can't do

Two more limits, both inconveniences. It can't see screen shares, only what's said and typed. And it answers in the chat, not out loud. This is version one. Notice that word.

### How fast this moves

Because this kind of thing moves fast. The one I use went from nothing, to a beta, to a first version, in a few months. Whatever I tell you it can't do today has a short shelf life.

### How to try it

So start now, while it's young. Don't put it on every meeting. Put it on one. The one where somebody always says: I'll check and get back to you.

Then tell me how it went. Which meeting, and one thing it caught that you'd have missed. One line is plenty.

Invite it. Admit it. See what it catches.
