## The lesson

Most people read the first answer and stop. Either they settle for it or give up on the tool. One follow-up line in the same chat usually turns a generic draft into a usable one, and asking the A.I. to interview you first is better still.

## The rule

Treat the first answer as a draft, not a verdict. Reply in the same conversation with one of three follow-ups: say specifically what's wrong, ask for a different shape, or give it something concrete to work from. For anything you'll care about, start by telling it: "Before you write it, ask me what you need to know." Don't open a new chat to fix a draft, because that throws away the context it has built.

This rule is written so an AI agent can follow it as a standing instruction.

## Transcript

Here's the simplest habit in this series, and almost nobody does it. You read the first answer, and you stop.

### The habit

Either you settle for what came back, even though it's not quite right. Or you decide the tool isn't very good, and do it yourself.

Watch. You ask in seven words. What comes back is fine. Generic, a bit stiff, not yours. Now add one line, same chat: too formal, half the length, lead with what changes for them. Now it's usable.

And be fair to it. You briefed it in seven words. You wouldn't accept that first draft from a colleague either. You'd tell them what to change.

### Three moves

There are only three kinds of second message. Say what's wrong, specifically. Ask for a different shape. Or hand it something real to work from. None of them takes longer than a sentence.

### The best one

But my favorite is the one I'd start with. Before it writes anything, tell it: ask me what you need to know. And it will. Who's reading this? What's changing for them? Do you want the reason, or just the change?

One sentence, and it stops guessing at what you meant. It asks. Exactly what you'd want from a person.

### One mistake

And when it isn't right, don't open a new chat. Everything it has worked out about what you want lives in this one. Starting over throws it all away.

The first answer isn't a verdict on whether this works. It's a draft. Treat it like one.

So go send the second message. It's the cheapest thing you'll do all day.
