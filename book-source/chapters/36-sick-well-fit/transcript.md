## Sick, Well, Fit

0:00 · `01_title` · 8.40s

Silicon Valley C Players. Chapter thirty-six. Sick, well, fit. How companies should use A.I.

## An Illustration

0:09 · `02_note` · 35.52s

An illustration, not a true story. Picture a made-up company that runs a billing system. Every dashboard is green. The site is up, tickets close on time, last month reconciles. By any reading, it is well. Then the board asks for A.I. in every team by the end of the quarter. An agent goes live on refunds. Nobody ever wrote down what a correct refund looks like, the one person who knew the edge cases is out that week, and the error counter starts to climb. Nothing was broken the day before. There was just no margin. That gap, between well and fit, is what this chapter is about.

## The Line

0:44 · `03_define` · 18.45s

In October two thousand two, Greg Glassman, the founder of CrossFit, published an essay called What Is Fitness? Championing a fitness program without clearly defining what it is that the program delivers, he wrote, combines elements of fraud and farce. So he defined it, in a way you could test.

1:03 · `04_line` · 28.51s

One of his observations was that nearly every measurable value of health can be placed on a continuum that ranges from sickness to wellness to fitness. His examples: a blood pressure of one sixty over ninety-five is pathological, one twenty over seventy is normal, and one oh five over fifty-five is consistent with an athlete. Body fat of forty percent is pathological, twenty percent is normal, ten percent is fit. Dozens of other measures line up the same way.

1:31 · `05_middle` · 12.07s

Done right, he wrote, fitness provides a great margin of protection against the ravages of time and disease. It is and should be super-wellness. Wellness is not the end of the scale. It is the middle.

1:43 · `06_aim` · 20.90s

Most people aim for well: numbers in the normal range. That is a reasonable goal if the alternative is being sick, and the wrong goal if you still have something hard to do. I think companies do the same thing. Most companies strive for well. I strive for fit. And the hard thing most companies now have to do is make A.I. part of how they actually operate.

2:04 · `07_thesis` · 18.16s

Here is what I believe about that, and it frames everything below. I strongly believe that A.I. is even more powerful to those that actually understand the fundamentals of the area they are applying A.I. to. Orders of magnitude more than the person vibing their way into a discipline A.I. gives them access to.

2:22 · `08_thesis_pair` · 13.27s

That is my view, not a measurement. I am not mocking the vibing: it gets a person, or a company, into a field they could not enter before. It gets them to well. Fundamentals plus A.I. is what gets them to fit.

## Sick, Well, Fit At Work

2:36 · `09_well` · 10.22s

Well is familiar. The site stays up most days. Tickets get closed. You can explain last month. It is better than sickness, and it is a thin place to stand.

2:46 · `10_sick` · 12.07s

Sickness you may have seen too. A number nobody can defend. A cutover nobody can roll back. A process that only works if one person is in the room. An A.I. pilot that nobody can say is working.

2:58 · `11_fit` · 8.11s

Fitness is a margin. When a vendor fails, a key person is out, or demand spikes, you still have room to move before you are in trouble.

## The Hedge

3:06 · `12_hedge` · 23.64s

In a later lecture, Glassman added the observation this chapter turns on. If you are fit, you first have to become well before you can become sick: fitness is a hedge against sickness, with wellness as an intermediate value. A fit company is less likely to go from fine to crisis in one step. It has to use up its margin first, and if it is reading its markers, it can watch that happen and act.

3:30 · `13_margin` · 20.28s

Chapter seventeen, Falling and Getting Up, is about getting back up after a failure. Margin is the room to do that. This is not a metaphor I am forcing onto a hobby. It is the best picture I have for what a company needs when the work ahead is hard and partly unknown. A.I. adoption is both. Well will not carry that. Fit might.

## Markers, Not Feelings

3:50 · `14_markers_a` · 32.18s

Medicine has lab values. A company has to choose its own markers. Here are five, with readings for sick, well, and fit. The readings are illustrations, not industry benchmarks. Choose your own, before you need them. Recovering from a bad release. Sick: days, and nobody is sure what changed. Well: hours, with a known rollback. Fit: minutes, with a rollback you have rehearsed. Who can run a core process. Sick: one person. Well: two people. Fit: several people, from a written runbook.

4:22 · `15_markers_b` · 26.08s

Where the numbers come from. Sick: argued about in meetings. Well: agreed this month. Fit: defined, sourced, and reproducible by someone else. Cash. Sick: a surprise. Well: budgeted. Fit: visible every week, with a plan for a bad quarter. When an A.I. agent says done. Sick: you take its word. Well: someone spot-checks. Fit: it shows evidence someone else can check.

## The Divergent Marker

4:48 · `16_divergent` · 30.04s

Glassman also offered a test. If anything in your life has one of these metrics moving in a wrong direction, he said, I want you to entertain the possibility you are doing something profoundly wrong. Under his program, he said, the markers improve together. Companies are good at averaging away the one marker that is going the wrong way. The agents ship more every week, so nobody looks at how much of it a person had to redo. Sometimes that is a trade you chose. Often nobody chose it. Find the divergent marker and investigate it first.

## Four Models, One Aim

5:18 · `17_four` · 30.04s

CrossFit does not stop at the line. Its Level One Training Guide uses four models at once so that fitness stays broad: ten general physical skills, the hopper, three metabolic pathways, and the continuum itself. The aim is to prepare people not only for the unknown but for the unknowable. The summary is five words long: our specialty is not specializing. Not specializing is a claim about capacity, not strategy. Specialize in what you sell, not in what you can survive.

## The Ten Skills

5:48 · `18_ten` · 23.64s

The first model is a list of ten: cardiovascular and respiratory endurance, stamina, strength, flexibility, power, speed, coordination, agility, balance, and accuracy. The test is blunt: You are as fit as you are competent in each of these ten skills. A company does not need ten separate translations. Four groups will do.

6:12 · `19_force` · 14.20s

Endurance and stamina are sustained load: can you repeat hard efforts without the quality dropping by week nine? Strength, power, and speed are force: can you move something heavy, and shorten the cycle of the work you repeat every week?

6:26 · `20_range` · 22.41s

Flexibility, agility, and coordination are range: a quick change of pattern when the plan changes, and functions that combine into one movement instead of separate drills. Balance and accuracy are control: staying over your support base, especially on cash, and landing where you aimed. If a report column is named one thing and counts another, you are not accurate.

6:49 · `21_practice` · 23.04s

The guide adds a distinction worth keeping. Some of the ten come from training, which changes the body. Coordination, agility, balance, and accuracy come from practice. A company builds some capacity the slow way, by changing its body: hiring, systems, A.I. tooling. Coordination and accuracy do not come that way. They come from doing the work together, many times.

## The Hopper

7:12 · `22_hopper` · 14.80s

The second model is a thought experiment. Load a lottery hopper with as many physical tasks as you can think of. Line everyone up, turn the handle, pull a task out at random, and test them. Whoever performs best across the random draws is the fittest.

7:27 · `23_two` · 23.32s

Two things follow. First, Glassman said, the fittest person on earth may be only in the seventy-fifth percentile at each event. Fit means good at everything before best at anything. Second, everyone knows the task they do not want to see come out of the hopper. That task is a chink in your armor, and there is more to gain from going after it than from more time on what you already do well.

7:50 · `24_co_hopper` · 25.17s

Every company has a hopper. A payment processor goes down on your busiest day. Your most important engineer resigns. A model or a vendor changes its behavior or its pricing overnight. An agent confidently reports done on something it could not reach. You will not get the list in advance, but you already know which draw you dread. Practice that one: a tabletop exercise, a game day, a restore you actually run.

8:15 · `25_cost` · 13.89s

The seventy-fifth percentile matters to whoever pays the bills. Margin is not free: a second person who can run payroll, cash held back. The model does not ask you to be the best at anything. It asks you not to have an event you cannot do at all.

## Three Engines

8:29 · `26_engines` · 20.28s

The third model is the three metabolic pathways that supply the energy for all human action. One dominates the highest-powered efforts, those lasting less than about ten seconds. One dominates moderate-powered efforts lasting up to several minutes. One dominates low-powered efforts that last longer. The guide wants competency in all three.

8:49 · `27_engines_co` · 23.95s

A company runs on three engines too. The hours engine handles an outage or a bad release. The weeks engine is a hard push to ship by a date. The years engine is a migration, or the slow work of making A.I. part of how you operate. Many companies favor one: great in a crisis and adrift in between, or good at long programs and frozen when the pager goes off. Train all three.

## Work Capacity

9:13 · `28_capacity` · 17.85s

The models led to a one-line definition of fitness: work capacity across broad time and modal domains. Plot power against duration. Fitness is the area under that curve, not one high point on it. Excellent at a planned launch and weak when something unknown shows up is narrow capacity.

9:31 · `29_health` · 19.68s

Then Glassman added a third axis: age. Extend the area under the curve across a life and you get a solid. That is health. For a company, the third axis is years: through a bad market, a migration, a new technology. A company that has one great year and then falls apart was not fit. It had a good season.

## The Prescription

9:51 · `30_rx` · 21.50s

The prescription that builds this capacity is short: constantly varied, high-intensity functional movement. Functional movements move large loads over long distances, quickly. Intensity is defined as power: work divided by time. The variation is there because preparing for unknown events is at odds with fixed, predictable, and routine regimens.

10:12 · `31_rx_work` · 21.81s

At work, functional means the movements that actually move the business: orders flowing, customers getting what they need, decisions landing, cash used on purpose. A standing meeting that never changes a decision is not a functional movement. Neither is an A.I. demo that never touches real work. High intensity does not mean panic. It means more real work finished per week.

10:34 · `32_routine` · 30.36s

Every regimen, every routine, Glassman wrote, contains within its structure a blueprint for its deficiency. Every operating rhythm hides a blind spot: the thing the weekly review never asks about. Keep the calendar and vary the questions. Rest is in the program too. The guide's general pattern is three days on and one day off, chosen for sustainability at high intensity. Recovery is part of the program, not a reward for finishing it. At work, that is slack you plan, not slack you hope for.

## Mechanics, Consistency, Intensity

11:04 · `33_mci` · 19.68s

There is a warning attached to intensity, and it is the one I want on the table before anyone hears go faster. CrossFit's charter for balancing safety, efficacy, and efficiency is mechanics, consistency, then, and only then, intensity. Ignoring the order increases the risk for injury and potentially blunts long-term progress.

11:24 · `34_ai_intensity` · 13.89s

This is where my belief about fundamentals pays off. A.I. is intensity. It amplifies whatever mechanics are already there, good or bad, and intensity on bad mechanics is how you get hurt. Intensity is also where the results come from.

11:38 · `34b_gap` · 27.60s

The person who knows the area can say what done means and check the work, so they can hand over more of it, with less watching. The person vibing cannot check, so they stay at low intensity or go fast blind. In my view that gap, not the tool, is where the difference in power comes from. So this is the order for rolling A.I. out across a company, and it is easy to run backward: a usage target for everyone, before anyone has said what done means.

12:06 · `35_mechanics` · 16.94s

Mechanics first. Before an agent does a job, you can say what it can reach, what done means for this task, and which number you are actually asking for. Two of the A.I. Tips on this site are about exactly this: Know What the Bot Can Actually Reach, and Ask for Proof, Not Done.

12:22 · `36_consistency` · 16.63s

Then consistency. The same task, repeated, passing the same check each time, without a hero in the room to rescue it. Only then intensity: more tasks, more teams, more volume, less supervision. Skip the order and you get a good demo and a bad week.

## Foundations First

12:39 · `37_pyramid` · 23.32s

The guide also draws a pyramid, a theoretical hierarchy of development for an athlete. From the bottom: nutrition, metabolic conditioning, gymnastics, weightlifting and throwing, and sport at the top. Then: We do not deliberately order these components but nature will. If you have a deficiency at any level of the pyramid the components above will suffer. Here is a company version.

13:02 · `38_pyr_base` · 21.81s

Nutrition is what you run on. Cash you can see and steer, data with definitions, and access that matches the job, for people and for agents. Software Engineering two oh one on this site has a lesson on agents that operate systems with the least access the job needs. Metabolic conditioning is the operating rhythm. The daily and weekly capacity to keep going.

13:24 · `39_pyr_top` · 19.08s

Gymnastics is control of your own body. Deploy, roll back, restore, and change your own process without waiting for outside help. Weightlifting is control of an external object. Vendors, partners, models, and the loads you pick up on purpose. Sport is the application. Out in the market, under competition.

13:43 · `40_pyr_ai` · 23.04s

A.I. sits near the top of that pyramid, with the other loads you pick up on purpose. It does not replace the base. The guide says the model is most useful for analyzing an athlete's shortcomings, and that is how to use it here: when something at the top keeps failing, look down. An A.I. program that keeps disappointing often has a data problem, an access problem, or nobody has defined what finished looks like.

## Virtuosity

14:06 · `41_virtuosity` · 26.68s

CrossFit borrows one more word from gymnastics: virtuosity, defined there as performing the common uncommonly well. In a two thousand five open letter to trainers, Glassman described what gets in its way. Novices in any art hurry past the fundamentals toward more elaborate techniques. He called that compulsion the novice's curse. Rushing ahead, he wrote, increases the chance of injury, delays advancement and progress.

14:33 · `42_curse_co` · 21.81s

Companies have their own version: the new tool, the new framework, the fleet of agents, adopted by a team that still cannot ship a clean release. Chapter six, Fundamentals Nobody Uses, is about principles that outlive tools. Chapter twenty-nine, The Ladder, treats A.I. coding as one more rung, from first principles up. A rung is no use without the ones under it.

14:55 · `43_virt_ai` · 16.94s

Virtuosity is also what makes A.I. safe to use. The person who knows the fundamentals can tell when the A.I.'s output is wrong, and steer it. A company that does the common things uncommonly well, a clean handoff, a number with a definition, a release it can roll back, is hard to knock over.

## Measurable, Observable, Repeatable

15:12 · `44_evidence` · 21.81s

CrossFit calls its approach evidence-based fitness. Meaningful statements about safety, efficacy, and efficiency, the guide says, can be supported only by measurable, observable, repeatable data. A score on the whiteboard counts. A feeling after the workout does not. Hold your own work to that. A bot that says done has not given you a result. Ask for proof.

15:34 · `45_predict` · 27.00s

In a twenty twenty-four talk, Glassman put the standard in one line: a model is a forecast of a measurement, and validation comes from predictive strength, period. A strategy is a model. So is an A.I. rollout plan. Write down the measurement you expect before you start, and grade the plan by whether it came true. Over several plans, you learn whose forecasts to trust. Trust works the same way: all rational trust, he said, is based on predictive strength.

16:01 · `46_goodhart` · 14.80s

Numbers have their own failure. Goodhart's law, in Marilyn Strathern's phrasing: When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. Train for one score and the rest of the curve suffers. Several markers are harder to game than one.

## Benchmarks

16:15 · `47_benchmark` · 28.22s

CrossFit measures progress with benchmark workouts, which hold the work constant. Fran is one: twenty-one, fifteen, and nine repetitions of ninety-five-pound thrusters and pull-ups. Every time you do Fran, the work is the same. So if you do it again a year later and finish faster, the difference in time is the difference in power. When Glassman introduced the benchmarks in two thousand three, he had them return through repeated, irregular, appearances, and asked everyone to post their results.

16:44 · `48_drills_a` · 21.81s

A company can keep benchmarks the same way. Here is a made-up subscription-billing company, with one made-up pair of times, to show the shape. Restore yesterday's ledger from backup into a clean environment. In the spring it took most of a day. By fall, under an hour. Ship a one-line change from an approved ticket to production, with tests and a rollback ready.

17:05 · `49_drills_b` · 24.86s

Have an agent reconcile one saved day of charges against the processor's report, and check its answer against the processor's totals, not its own summary. Close the month with numbers someone else can reproduce. Time them every quarter, unannounced where you can, and publish the times. The comparison holds only while the work really is the same, so write the drill down. A benchmark nobody has run lately is a memory.

## Scale the Load

17:30 · `50_scale` · 16.03s

The guide makes a claim small teams should hear: Your needs and the Olympic athlete's differ by degree not kind. The principle for scaling a workout is to preserve the stimulus: adjust load, speed, and volume so the workout has a similar effect on each person.

17:46 · `51_scale_co` · 13.27s

A five-person company does not need an enterprise disaster-recovery program. It needs to restore its database once, on purpose, and time it. It does not need an A.I. governance board. It needs to know what each agent can reach.

## Fitness Plus Luck

18:00 · `52_luck` · 26.68s

None of this guarantees anything. In lectures given in twenty sixteen, Glassman put the outcome as a sum: fitness plus luck equals health. By luck he meant accidents, the things you can do nothing about. The outcome is the part you can do something about plus the part you can do nothing about. You cannot train luck away, and a hard enough hit can end a fit company. What fitness changes is the odds: more of the bad draws cost you some margin instead of the company.

## The Sport of Fitness

18:26 · `53_sport` · 22.12s

The last piece is how CrossFit gets people to work this hard. In implementation, the guide says, CrossFit is quite simply, a sport: the Sport of Fitness. The camaraderie and competition of sport yield an intensity that cannot be matched by other means. So it uses whiteboards as scoreboards, keeps accurate scores, runs a clock, and defines the standards precisely.

18:48 · `54_open` · 25.77s

It is also built in the open. We are an open-source fitness program, Glassman wrote in two thousand five, in an article that lists the innovations that came directly from our clients. And the gyms are affiliates, not franchises. A business profile in the CrossFit Journal quoted him on why: if I try to get everyone moving in lockstep, I get everyone moving towards mediocrity. Left to vary, he said, best practices arise at a breathtaking rate.

19:14 · `55_spread` · 23.32s

Half of that is a sound way to spread A.I. through a company: the use cases come from the teams, and from customers. The other half a company has to add from the center: shared standards, open scores, and common guardrails. Score the benchmarks, not the usage, or the scoreboard becomes the target. And train together. Fitness in one department and sickness in another is not a fit company.

## Well Is a Reading. Fit Is a Margin.

19:37 · `56_close` · 18.76s

I am not asking anyone to join a CrossFit class. I am asking you not to confuse well with fit. Well is a normal reading. Fit is a margin for when something goes wrong. If you train only to be well, you spend the hard days finding out how little room you had. If you train to be fit, you have already built the buffer.

## Place Your Team on the Line

19:56 · `57_questions` · 31.56s

To place your team on the line, do not start with a feeling. Pick two or three markers you can observe. Would someone else get the same number from your sources? Can the core work run for a week if its usual owner is out? If an A.I. tool or a vendor disappeared tomorrow, how long before the work stops? If the same team did twice the volume next month, with A.I. doing the extra, what would break first? Which marker is moving the wrong way while the others improve? Which draw from the hopper do you dread, and when did you last practice it?

20:28 · `58_readings` · 13.89s

Sick: you cannot answer, or the answer is: only if this one person is here. Well: you can answer, and the week usually works. Fit: you can lose a person, a vendor, or a week of luck and still deliver, and you can show the evidence.

## 100 Words

20:42 · `59_glassman_100` · 47.71s

Glassman's two thousand two essay carried a box that put his whole method into a hundred words: Eat meat and vegetables, nuts and seeds, some fruit, little starch and no sugar. Keep intake to levels that will support exercise but not body fat. Practice and train major lifts: Deadlift, clean, squat, presses, clean and jerk, and snatch. Similarly, master the basics of gymnastics: pull-ups, dips, rope climb, push-ups, sit-ups, presses to handstand, pirouettes, flips, splits, and holds. Bike, run, swim, row, et cetera, hard and fast. Five or six days per week mix these elements in as many combinations and patterns as creativity will allow. Routine is the enemy. Keep workouts short and intense. Regularly learn and play new sports.

## The Rule

21:56 · `61_rule` · 17.23s

The rule. Well is a reading and fit is a margin, so train for fit. Choose markers you can observe, time the same work every quarter, and practice the event you dread. Put A.I. on fundamentals you understand: mechanics first, then consistency, and only then intensity.

22:13 · `62_rule_full_a` · 30.36s

In full: when you report how a team, a system, or an agent's work is doing, do not start with a feeling. Name the markers, give each a reading of sick, well, or fit, and show evidence someone else can check. If one marker is moving the wrong way while the others improve, say that first, and investigate it first. Before you start a plan, write down the measurement you expect, and afterward say whether it came true. Keep a short list of benchmark work, hold the work the same, time it every quarter, and post the times.

22:44 · `63_rule_full_b` · 23.64s

Name the event you least want to draw, and rehearse it. Turn A.I., scope, or speed up only where three things hold: someone understands the fundamentals well enough to tell when the output is wrong; the mechanics are right; and the result passes the same check without a hero. Scale the load to the size of the team and keep the movement. Plan the slack. Fitness improves the odds. It does not remove bad luck.

## Business Fitness in 100 Words

24:06 · `67_business_100` · 42.52s

Business Fitness in one hundred Words. Spend on customers and product, people and tools, a little on overhead, nothing on vanity. Keep cash at levels that fund growth but not waste. Practice the core movements: build, sell, serve, collect. Master the basics: clean data, clear owners, written decisions, honest numbers. Ship, test, learn and fix, hard and fast. Every week, mix these in as many combinations as the work demands. Routine is the enemy; process is not. Keep projects short and intense. Put A.I. in every movement, mechanics first. Regularly take on problems you've never seen. Measure it, or it didn't happen.
