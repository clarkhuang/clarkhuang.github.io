# Naming and fictionalization policy

*Silicon Valley C Players* is an **embellished fictional dramatization**. This policy is how every writer — human or AI agent — keeps it that way. (The real→fictional map is private and is not in this repo. Agents should never ask for it or guess at it.)

## Two genres, clearly labeled

| Part | Genre | Real names allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| Foreword, OpenBook frame, "The story is still being written" | Nonfiction (the author speaking as himself) | The author only |
| Prologue, Parts One–Four, Interlude, Epilogue | **Fiction** (embellished dramatization) | **No.** Fictional narrator, people, companies, schools, venues |
| Part Five: How to Work Now, Appendix: The Rules | Nonfiction (opinions + cited public sources) | Real *public sources* (papers, surveys, companies cited for public facts), with links |

## Rules for the fiction chapters

1. **Narrator:** Charlie Hack (initials C.H.), a fictional C player loosely inspired by the author.
2. **Every private person is renamed *and* de-identified.** Change at least two of: job/role, nationality/hometown, gender/age, appearance, year, city. Prefer composites.
3. **Every company, product, school, and venue is fictional.** Don't use real company names, logos, product names, patent numbers, ticker symbols, IPO details, deal terms, or real headcounts/revenues.
4. **Public figures** are replaced by fictional stand-ins with no "tells" (no signature products, catchphrases, looks, or famous dates).
5. **Negative or embarrassing conduct** (drinking, drugs, sex, crimes, cruelty, insults, firing, incompetence) goes only to fictional or composite characters — or to the narrator.
6. **No confidential material**, fictional or not: no systems internals, security, customer data, financials, deal terms, or anything under an NDA.
7. **Exaggeration should be obvious.** Comic hyperbole a reader won't take literally beats a plausible-sounding false fact.
8. **Places** (San Francisco, Berlin) can be settings; move specific venues for any embarrassing scene.
9. **No "guess who" winks**, in the text or in marketing. The game is "could that have happened?", never "who is this really?"
10. **Contributor submissions** are fictionalized the same way before they enter any chapter, even if the contributor said "you can use my name."

## Pre-publication checklist (every book PR)

- [ ] No real names of private people, companies, schools, products, or venues in fiction chapters
- [ ] Each character drawn from a real person has ≥2 identifying details changed or is a composite
- [ ] No negative/embarrassing conduct attached to a traceable real person or company
- [ ] No confidential, NDA, customer, security, deal, or financial details
- [ ] No public-figure names or tells in fiction chapters or teasers
- [ ] No minors' identifying details; family content only with Clark's opt-in
- [ ] Nonfiction claims in Part Five are sourced and dated
- [ ] Open `takedown-request` issues touching this chapter are resolved
