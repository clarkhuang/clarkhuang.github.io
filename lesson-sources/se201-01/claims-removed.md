# Claims from the Mac draft removed or toned down, and why

| Mac draft (scene) | What happened | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Title sub and outro sub carried a company name | Removed. The title sub is now "From loop to enterprise systems • clarkhuang.github.io" and the outro sub is "Software Engineering 201". | No company name outside the public career record may appear. The new subs follow the recorded lessons' conventions. |
| `02_quiz` "what was scarcest thirty years ago? ... I'd say the machines." | Replaced by a quiz about now, with a defensible answer ("knowing it's right"). | An answer of "I'd say" is the scriptwriter's opinion presented as Clark's. A quiz about now is a stronger hook and sets up the lesson. |
| `03_hook` "I've watched the way you build a big one change three times." | Toned down to "the way you build a big system keeps changing." | "Exactly three times" is the scriptwriter's framing presented as Clark's observation. The four eras are kept as a lens instead. |
| `03_hook` "Investment tools." | Changed to "an investment adviser for nonprofits" (eCIO's public description). | The public record says eCIO is an investment adviser. "Tools" goes beyond it. |
| `04_thesis` "Here's the pattern I see. In every era, the architecture bends..." | Changed to "Here's a useful way to see why", shown on screen as "A useful lens". | The scriptwriter wrote the thesis, so it is framed as a teaching lens, not Clark's long-held belief. |
| `05_1996` "And companies traded data in overnight batch files." | Kept, with the qualifier "through E.D.I., and much of that still runs". "The web was brand new" became "the commercial web was brand new". | EDI and batch processing are still widespread, and the web itself dates from about 1991. Experienced engineers would object otherwise. |
| `06_2006` "the first cloud services had just appeared" | Changed to "the first big public cloud services had just launched". | Hosted and online services existed before 2006. The public IaaS launches (S3, EC2) are what's meant. |
| `07_2016` "The cloud, by default." / "Many small services" | Changed to "by default, for new systems" and "owned by small teams". | Enterprises were not cloud-by-default in 2016. "One team per service" is naive. |
| `07_2016` scarce = "Attention" | Changed to "Seeing the whole", with observability (dashboards, tracing) as the response. | "Attention" was vague. The draft's own explanation ("nobody could hold the whole system in their head") is about seeing the whole, and the observability response is historically accurate. |
| `08_now` "Agents write much of the code." | Changed to "Agents can write much of the code." | It is not true everywhere, so the claim is softened. |
| `09_design` "That changes how I'd design a system today." | Reframed as a pictured subscription-billing system, labelled an illustration, then the caveat. The team line is now exactly as the facts file states: "one repository, with instruction files for the agents". | This avoids presenting the scriptwriter's design list as Clark's settled method, and avoids implying sticky.io's real design. |
| `10_caveat` "Ten years ago, the answer was to split everything into small services." | Replaced by "Every era had a confident answer, then found its costs", plus "the evidence for it is still thin, and indirect" (round 1 said "at least one source..."; round 2 found an unnamed source evasive). | "Split everything" overgeneralizes. The facts file says the stance is "assembled from indirect evidence". |
| `11_constants` example "a cloud" (narration of the dependency constant) | Changed to "a bank, a card network, a partner's system". | A constant "true in all four eras" can't use a 1996 example that didn't exist yet. |
| `12_plan` "Each lesson takes one of those constants, and one real system I've worked on. What we built. What I'd keep..." | Removed. Replaced by the actual nine-lesson path. | It no longer matches the curriculum. Recorded lessons 2-4 use this site as the example. It would also promise internal accounts of company systems that the facts don't support. |
| `13_rule_intro` "As in one oh one, each lesson ends with a rule for our agents." | Removed. The rule now opens with "Which gives us rule one, for your agents", as in lessons 2-4. | This matches the recorded convention and saves time. "Our agents" read as internal, and the public lessons say "your agents". |
| `14_rule` "For our agents." | Changed to "for your agents". A sixth line was added: "Put it in the repo, where every agent can read it." | It is a public audience. The extra line ties the rule to lesson 2's "readable system". |
| `15_ask` "Reply with it ... I'll tell you which era's thinking is hiding inside it." | Changed to: write it down yourself, then ask your AI which era's thinking is in it. | The site has no per-viewer reply channel, so the promise could not be kept. |
| Mac spec `id` "se201-01", output "../Software Engineering 201/01-four-eras/...", slug "Four-Eras" | Changed to id `se201-01-four-eras` and output `01-four-eras-of-building-systems/SE201_01_Four-Eras-Of-Building-Systems_public.mp4`. | These are the required ids and paths. |
| `se201_layouts1.py` eras card (h 560, 3 rows) | Replaced by `se201_layouts_eras.py`: taller cards (h 700), 4 rows, every string auto-shrunk to fit. | In the draft, the third two-line row ran into the "what was scarce" box (rows ended around y+488 while the box started at y+448). |
